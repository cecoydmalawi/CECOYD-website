Who We Are

About CECOYD

A local non-governmental, non-profit and non-political organisation committed to improving the socio-economic status of rural youth in Malawi.

Centre for Concerned Youth in Development (CECOYD) was established in 2014 and registered under the Trustees Incorporation Act in 2016. We work primarily in Traditional Authority Kalolo, Lilongwe District, focusing on young people as they enter adulthood.

Our approach is rooted in the belief that when youth are equipped with education, skills, rights awareness and economic opportunities, they become powerful agents of positive change for their families, communities and the nation.

We focus on seven critical areas: Education, Health, Agriculture & Entrepreneurship, Environment, Democracy, Human Rights and Participation.

With a strong, gender-sensitive organisational structure and over 40 active youth clubs (each with 30–60 members), CECOYD continues to deliver practical, community-owned solutions.

🎯 Our Vision

Youth who are socially and economically empowered and can effectively contribute positively to the social-economic activities of their families, communities and nation at large.

🚀 Our Mission

To ensure vibrant youth who participate in all aspects of socio-economic development through collaboration with other development stakeholders — by focusing on education, health, agriculture, environment, democracy, human rights and participation.

📍 Where We Work

Offices are located 1.5 km after Msundwe Trading Centre (just before the twin transmitter towers). Msundwe is 35 km from Lilongwe City along the Lilongwe–Mchinji Road, in T/A Kalolo.

What We Do

Our Flagship Programmes

YESE opens doors through education. YEEM builds lasting economic independence through sustainable agriculture. Together they create a complete pathway for rural youth.

2017 – 2026 · Education Pathway

YESE

Youth Economic Empowerment through Supportive Education

What YESE Does

  • Identifies and supports vulnerable children and youth back into school
  • Provides school fees, uniforms, groceries, learning materials and scholarships
  • Offers mentorship, psychosocial support and life-skills development
  • Conducts community awareness on child marriage and school dropout
  • Strengthens Youth-Friendly Health Services
  • Tracks beneficiaries from primary through to tertiary completion

2022 – 2026 · Economic Pathway

YEEM

Youth Economic Empowerment through Sustainable Agriculture

From Seed to Prosperity

  • Mobilises and registers young farmers into youth clubs
  • Delivers technical training by LUANAR (agronomy, business & financial literacy)
  • Supplies certified soybean seed, inoculant, pesticides and farm support
  • Provides continuous on-farm inspection and extension advice
  • Organises collective harvesting, storage, bulk marketing and value addition
  • Disburses profits transparently and builds youth enterprises

How We Work

Our Delivery Models

Clear, structured pathways that turn support into lasting results for every young person we serve.

YESE Model Education

1

Receiving Request

Families, schools or community leaders refer vulnerable children and youth.

2

Identification & Assessment

Thorough assessment of need, vulnerability and commitment to education.

3

Recruitment

Selected beneficiaries are formally enrolled into the YESE programme.

4

School Needs + Guidance

Fees, materials, uniforms, counselling and continuous mentorship provided.

5

Monitoring & Evaluation

Regular follow-up on attendance, performance and well-being.

6

Graduation & Transition

Successful completion and linkage to higher education or economic opportunities.

YEEM Model Agriculture

1

Youth Mobilisation

Identify, mobilise and register young farmers into the programme.

2

Capacity Building

Training in agronomy, agribusiness and financial literacy by LUANAR.

3

Inputs & Production

Certified seed, inoculant and ongoing technical farm support.

4

Harvest & Aggregation

Collective collection, quality control and storage in a central warehouse.

5

Marketing & Value Addition

Bulk selling to processors plus processing into soy flour, milk, oil & feed.

6

Profit & Empowerment

Transparent profit sharing and building of sustainable youth enterprises.

Results That Matter

Our Impact

Real change in education access, protection of girls, youth skills, income and community leadership across Traditional Authority Kalolo.

📚

Education Access

More than 200 children and young people returned to school since 2017. Scholarships now support students at UNIMA, LUANAR, MUST, Mzuni, MUBAS and multiple technical colleges.

🛡️

Child Protection

300 girls and boys rescued from early marriage. 15,000 community members reached with civic education. 56 civic educators trained to sustain the work.

🌱

Economic Empowerment

Young farmers trained in modern soybean production and value addition. Collective marketing and agribusiness skills are creating real income pathways.

🤝

Youth Leadership

Over 40 active youth clubs with 30–60 members each. Continuous capacity building for club leaders and peer educators across the area.

⚖️

Rights & Democracy

Active partner in the Positive Masculinity Platform for gender equality. Encouraging youth political participation and holding leaders accountable.

🌍

Environment & Health

Tree-planting campaigns, environmental clean-ups and SRHR education through youth clubs and local health centres.
